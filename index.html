Haiming Li is a health economist and a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at Lehigh University, where he expects to complete his degree in May 2027. His research asks what happens on the other side of a policy boundary. A law is written for one state, an insurance plan covers some services and not others, a market is reshaped for one kind of buyer — but people, and their health, cross those lines. The costs often land on populations the policy never targeted.

His job market paper follows recreational cannabis legalization in New Jersey across the state line into Pennsylvania. Related work traces the same question across substances (e-cigarette regulation and cannabis), across coverage lines (from oral health to heart disease and cardiac spending), through the loss of care capacity (psychiatric hospital closures under Pennsylvania's CHIPP program), and across markets (vehicle-market restructuring and rural labor supply).

He is on the job market in the 2026–27 academic year.

Job Market Paper

Cross-Border Mental Health Effects of Cannabis Legalization Policy: Evidence of Spillovers from New Jersey to Pennsylvania

Psychiatric hospital admissions rose by roughly 8 percent in Pennsylvania ZIP codes within driving range of New Jersey dispensaries, relative to matched ZIP codes farther away — a cross-border cost that assessments of legalization usually leave out.
