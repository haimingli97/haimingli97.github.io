I am a Ph.D. candidate in Business and Economics at Lehigh University and will complete my degree in May 2027. My research uses quasi-experimental methods and large-scale administrative and survey data to study health policy, with a focus on substance use and mental health.

My current research falls into three areas: 1) the spillover effects of substance-use policy, including how cannabis legalization and e-cigarette regulation affect mental health and health care use in states that did not change their own laws; 2) mental health care capacity, including whether closing state psychiatric hospitals shifts the burden of care onto acute care hospitals; and 3) the downstream costs of poor oral health, including whether dental coverage reduces the financial burden of heart disease among older adults. I have presented this work at the American College of Medical Genetics Annual Meeting and the New York State Economics Association, and will present it at the Southern Economic Association and APPAM meetings in November 2026.

I have taught Intermediate Microeconomic Analysis as instructor of record and will teach Econometric Software in Spring 2027. I have also worked as a teaching assistant for principles of economics, statistical methods, and introduction to finance. Outside health economics, I have research experience in labor economics and teaching experience in econometrics and finance.

I am on the academic job market in 2026-27.

Job Market Paper

Cross-Border Spillovers of Recreational Cannabis Legalization on Mental Health: Evidence from Pennsylvania

After New Jersey legalized recreational cannabis in 2021, psychiatric hospitalizations in Pennsylvania ZIP codes within two hours of its dispensaries rose by about 8 percent compared with matched ZIP codes farther away. Evaluations of legalization that stop at the state line miss this cost.
