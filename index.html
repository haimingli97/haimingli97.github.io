I am a Ph.D. candidate in Business and Economics at Lehigh University and will complete my degree in May 2027. My research uses quasi-experimental methods and large-scale administrative and survey data to study health policy, with a focus on substance use and mental health.

My current research falls into three areas: 1) the spillover effects of substance-use policy — how cannabis legalization and e-cigarette regulation affect mental health and health care use, including in neighboring states that did not change their own laws; 2) mental health care capacity — how the closure of state psychiatric hospitals affects mental health care use and crime; and 3) the downstream costs of poor oral health — whether it raises heart-disease-related spending among older adults, and what this implies for adding a dental benefit to Medicare. My work has been presented at the American College of Medical Genetics Annual Meeting and the New York State Economics Association, and will be presented at the Southern Economic Association and APPAM meetings in November 2026.

I have taught Intermediate Microeconomic Analysis as instructor of record and will teach Econometric Software in Spring 2027. I have also served as a teaching assistant for principles of economics, statistical methods, and introduction to finance. Beyond health economics, I have research experience in labor economics and teaching experience in econometrics and finance.

I am on the academic job market in 2026–27.

Job Market Paper

Cross-Border Mental Health Effects of Cannabis Legalization Policy: Evidence of Spillovers from New Jersey to Pennsylvania

After New Jersey legalized recreational cannabis in 2021, psychiatric hospitalizations in Pennsylvania ZIP codes within two hours of its dispensaries rose by about 8 percent relative to matched ZIP codes farther away — a cost that evaluations stopping at the state line leave out.
