I am a health economist and a Ph.D. candidate in Business and Economics at Lehigh University, and I expect to complete my degree in May 2027. My research asks what happens on the other side of a policy boundary. A law is written for one state, an insurance plan covers some services and not others, a market is reshaped for one kind of buyer — but people, and their health, cross those lines. The costs often land on people the policy never targeted.

My job market paper follows recreational cannabis legalization in New Jersey across the state line into Pennsylvania. My other work traces the same question across substances (e-cigarette regulation and cannabis), across coverage lines (from oral health to heart disease and cardiac spending), through the loss of care capacity (psychiatric hospital closures under Pennsylvania's CHIPP program), and across markets (vehicle-market restructuring and rural labor supply).

I am on the academic job market in 2026–27.

Job Market Paper

Cross-Border Mental Health Effects of Cannabis Legalization Policy: Evidence of Spillovers from New Jersey to Pennsylvania

Psychiatric hospital admissions rose by roughly 8 percent in Pennsylvania ZIP codes within driving range of New Jersey dispensaries, relative to matched ZIP codes farther away — a cross-border cost that assessments of legalization usually leave out.
