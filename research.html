Job Market Paper

Cross-Border Mental Health Effects of Cannabis Legalization Policy: Evidence of Spillovers from New Jersey to Pennsylvania

Recreational cannabis legalization has spread state by state, so that residents of states that still prohibit sales often live within driving distance of a legal market. This paper asks whether New Jersey's legalization of recreational cannabis in 2021 affected psychiatric hospitalizations in neighboring Pennsylvania, which permits only medical use. I combine ZIP code–quarter inpatient discharge records for 2018–2022 with driving times from each Pennsylvania ZIP code to the nearest New Jersey dispensaries, and compare matched ZIP codes in counties near and far from those dispensaries before and after legalization. After legalization, psychiatric hospitalizations in ZIP codes in counties within two hours of New Jersey dispensaries were higher than in matched distant ZIP codes by 1.07 per 1,000 residents per quarter, about 8 percent of the control mean. The difference is concentrated in depression, alcohol- and tobacco-related disorders, and dementia and other organic disorders; cannabis-coded hospitalizations and psychosis show no comparable change. Cost–benefit assessments of legalization that stop at the state line omit a cost that falls on neighboring states.

Working Papers

Fueling Out of Work: Vehicle Market Restructuring and Labor Supply among Car-Dependent Workers

with Yizhao Wang · Under review

The 2011 reform of Corporate Average Fuel Economy standards set footprint-based targets that were less stringent for larger vehicles, and U.S. production shifted away from compact cars toward SUVs and light trucks, thinning the supply of entry-level vehicles. Using CPS ASEC data for 2001–2019 and a rural–urban difference-in-differences design, we find that rural labor force participation and employment fell by about 0.9 percentage points after the reform, with larger declines among low- and middle-income adults, while usual hours rose among those who stayed employed. The pattern is consistent with a fixed-cost channel: where a private vehicle is the only way to reach a job, restructuring the vehicle market shows up in labor supply.

The Causal Effect of Poor Oral Health on Heart Disease: A GWAS-by-Subtraction Genomic Structural Equation Instrumental Variables Model

with Chad D. Meyerhoefer, Erin Ware, John Moeller, Haiyan Chen, Jessica Faul, and Richard Manski

We estimate the causal effect of total tooth loss on heart disease using genetic and survey data from the Health and Retirement Study, 2006–2020. A GWAS-by-subtraction procedure constructs a polygenic score instrument purged of variants with direct effects on heart disease. The instrumental variables estimates are substantially larger than the corresponding OLS associations and stronger for men, which implies that cost projections for a Medicare dental benefit are overstated when they ignore offsetting reductions in heart disease treatment.
